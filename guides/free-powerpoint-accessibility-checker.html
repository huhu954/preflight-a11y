Free PowerPoint accessibility checker: what it can and cannot find

By Preflight A11y · 29 October 2026 · 6 minute read

If you search for a “free PowerPoint accessibility checker”, you will find three kinds of tools: the built-in Microsoft Accessibility Checker, browser-based scanners, and add-ins that overlay a toolbar on the slides. They are not equivalent.

This page explains what a free checker can realistically detect in a PPTX file, what needs a person, and how to run a browser-only preflight without uploading your deck.

Short version: free checkers are good at presence/structure checks (titles, alt text present, table headers, empty links). They are weak at meaning, reading order, contrast in context, and screen-reader experience. A clean report is not a certificate.

What free checkers usually miss

  • Whether alt text is meaningful. “Chart” or “image1.png” may pass a presence check and fail a learner.
  • Reading order. Screen readers follow object order, not visual position. A checker can flag a likely mismatch, but a person must verify it in the Selection Pane and with a screen reader.
  • Contrast in context. A checker can sample colors, but gradients, images behind text, and theme variations need review.
  • Chart data. The alt text should convey the takeaway, not just the chart type.
  • Caption quality. Auto-captions may miss names, acronyms, and technical vocabulary.
  • Whether the deck makes sense. No tool can judge pedagogical clarity.
