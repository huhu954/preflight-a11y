Accessibility preflight vs audit: what should you buy?

By Preflight A11y · 29 October 2026 · 6 minute read

“We need an accessibility audit” often means one of four different purchases: a preflight, an audit, remediation, or a certification. They have different scopes, different evidence, and very different prices. Confusing them is how teams end up with a clean scanner report and an inaccessible course.

Rule of thumb: preflight is triage; audit is evidence; remediation is the fix; certification is a claim made by a qualified third party. A scanner is not any of the last three.

The four deliverables

DeliverableQuestion it answersTypical outputWhat it does not prove
PreflightWhich files likely have critical barriers, and where should we start?Scored list, severity, locations, fix hints, ownerFull conformance or a legal defence
AuditDoes this document meet the agreed standard, and what is the evidence?Sampled manual tests, screen-reader notes, WCAG mapping, exceptionsThat future edits will stay accessible
RemediationCan you fix the file and return an accessible version?Updated DOCX/PPTX/PDF plus a change logThat the fix is complete without a re-test
Certification / VPATCan a qualified party attest to conformance?Signed report or vendor conformance reportThat your course files are covered

Why preflight is worth doing first

A preflight is cheap and fast. It answers the scheduling question: do we have three critical files or three hundred? It also gives an instructional designer a concrete worklist before an expert’s time is spent on manual testing.

For DOCX and PPTX, a useful preflight checks document title, language, heading structure, slide titles, alt-text presence and quality signals, table headers, ambiguous links, text boxes, hidden slides, tiny text, tracked changes, comments, and embedded media. That is enough to separate “quick fix” from “needs a specialist”.

When a preflight is not enough

Buy an audit when the document is part of a procurement, a legal response, a public-facing course, or a recurring template that many people will use. An audit should include manual testing, not just a scanner:

  • Keyboard-only navigation of interactive content.
  • Screen-reader spot checks (VoiceOver, NVDA, or JAWS) on representative files.
  • Reading-order and alt-text quality review by a person.
  • Caption/transcript review for technical vocabulary and names.
  • PDF tag/reading-order checks if the PDF is the deliverable.

When remediation is the right purchase

If the file is the deliverable and the team does not have the skills or time to fix it, buy remediation. Ask for the updated file, a change log, and a re-test. “We ran a scanner” is not remediation.

What about a VPAT?

A VPAT is a vendor’s conformance report for a product, usually for procurement. It is not a document-level audit of your syllabus, slide deck, or PDF. If a supplier sends a VPAT, ask which version, which standard, which product version, and which exceptions apply.

Questions to ask a vendor

  1. What standard and version are you testing against (WCAG 2.0, 2.1, 2.2; A/AA)?
  2. Which parts are automated, and which are manual?
  3. Do you test with a screen reader? Which one?
  4. What is the sampling method for a 100-file course?
  5. What do you deliver: findings only, fixed files, or both?
  6. What do you explicitly not cover?
  7. Will you re-test after remediation?
  8. Are you calling the result “compliant” or “preflighted”?
